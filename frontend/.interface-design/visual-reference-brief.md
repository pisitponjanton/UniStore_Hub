## 1. Source policy

These references are visual inspiration only. They are not templates to reproduce pixel-for-pixel.

Use them to learn:

- composition and hierarchy,
- card geometry and spacing,
- use of product/3D imagery,
- color relationships,
- navigation weight,
- surface layering,
- typography scale,
- motion opportunities,
- and responsive collapse behavior.

Do **not** copy:

- logos,
- brand names,
- proprietary illustrations,
- exact page compositions,
- exact marketing copy,
- testimonials,
- fake metrics,
- ratings/review counts,
- or data that UniStore Hub does not actually have.

If a canonical URL becomes unavailable later, use the written analysis in this document and record that the source is unavailable. Do not silently replace it with a different image.

## 3. Reference R1 — dark premium SaaS landing

**URL:** https://i.pinimg.com/736x/d5/32/e3/d532e399c402236d1bea894e08300975.jpg

### Visual signature

A premium landing composition with a dark indigo hero, large rounded container, purple accent color, a strong human/visual focal point, then lighter content sections below. The page mixes a high-contrast hero with clean pricing/feature blocks.

### Composition

- Hero occupies a large visual block instead of a narrow text header.
- Copy and dominant visual share the same hero rather than living in separate generic cards.
- Following sections are visually grouped into large rounded zones.
- Pricing/features use repeated cards, but the hierarchy is broken up by stronger section-level framing.
- The page uses generous vertical rhythm and clear transitions between dark and light areas.

### Card geometry and spacing

- Large outer radius.
- Medium/large internal card radius.
- Cards sit on a soft background rather than being defined mainly by thin borders.
- Spacing is generous enough for the surfaces to feel premium instead of administrative.

### Color/material

- Near-black/navy-indigo hero.
- Saturated violet/purple for emphasis and CTA.
- Pale lavender/neutral supporting surfaces.
- Depth comes from contrast, soft shadows, and layered zones rather than hard outlines.

### Typography/navigation

- Large, heavy display headline.
- Short supporting copy.
- Compact top navigation.
- CTA has strong contrast and visual weight.
- Body copy remains smaller and quieter.

### Imagery / dimensional treatment

- A large foreground visual creates personality and scale.
- Abstract dimensional background shapes support the visual without competing with the main CTA.

### Motion cues

Static reference only; no motion is directly verified. Suitable adaptation would be subtle CTA/visual hover and a short reveal, not continuous movement.

### Responsive implication

On mobile:

- hero should become a stacked composition,
- visual should not push the CTA below an excessive fold,
- cards should collapse to one column,
- large display text needs responsive clamp sizing.

### Adopt

- large branded hero zone,
- strong dark/light section contrast where appropriate,
- bold purple/indigo anchor,
- large rounded composition,
- high-quality illustration/product focal point,
- section-level visual rhythm.

### Adapt for UniStore

- replace generic SaaS marketing with real campus marketplace/store/campaign content,
- use product/store/package/campus visuals rather than stock business portrait imagery,
- keep Thai copy readable and shorter,
- use backend-driven commerce facts rather than fake marketing metrics.

### Reject

- fake customer counts,
- fake trust numbers,
- SaaS pricing-page patterns where they do not map to UniStore,
- stock-photo-led brand identity.

## 4. Reference R2 — vibrant orange product marketplace

**URL:** https://i.pinimg.com/1200x/34/c7/99/34c799e0775ef700eb11cbe36f7cc0e3.jpg

### Visual signature

A product-led commerce page using a warm orange/yellow environment, oversized product images, rounded product cards, floating presentation, and playful asymmetry.

### Composition

- Product grid is the primary visual object.
- A large colored background block sits behind the cards, creating depth without needing many separators.
- Product cards overlap or visually float over the section background.
- Navigation is lightweight compared with the product imagery.
- Secondary labels/settings remain small so products dominate.

### Card geometry and spacing

- Tall product cards with large radius.
- Product image occupies most of the card.
- Information is intentionally brief: title, price, small actions.
- Generous gaps preserve the premium/friendly appearance.
- Background layers and card elevation create separation.

### Color/material

- Saturated orange/yellow base.
- White/cream cards.
- Product visuals introduce blue/green/purple contrast.
- Soft shadows and subtle warm gradients add dimension.

### Typography/navigation

- Centered page title is bold but not oversized.
- Product titles/prices are concise.
- Top nav is narrow and low visual weight.

### Imagery / dimensional treatment

- Product image is the hero of each card.
- Strong cutout/3D product presentation.
- Simple colored graphic blocks behind product images increase visual energy.

### Motion cues

Static reference only. For UniStore, use a very small hover lift/image scale and press feedback, with reduced-motion fallback.

### Responsive implication

- 3-column desktop should become 2-column tablet then 1-column/safe 2-column mobile depending on content width.
- Decorative side labels should disappear or reflow.
- Product artwork must stay within card bounds.
- Primary product action must remain touch-safe.

### Adopt

- image-first product cards,
- vivid commerce color blocks,
- large radii,
- layered product/card presentation,
- concise product metadata,
- stronger product photography/illustration ratio.

### Adapt for UniStore

- support products without images using branded category placeholders,
- preserve actual store/campaign/variant context,
- show authoritative price/state/actions only,
- use color families per category/campaign sparingly.

### Reject

- decorative controls with no meaning,
- fixed desktop-only side text,
- fake favorite/cart counts,
- product imagery that hides required status or variant availability.

## 5. Reference R3 — soft 3D bento category system

**URL:** https://i.pinimg.com/736x/e1/c4/4e/e1c44e2ed5030037cfd8b4e42b153ef9.jpg

### Visual signature

A compact bento composition made of rounded colored blocks and soft 3D/clay objects. Each category uses a different pastel/bright color while the overall system remains cohesive.

### Composition

- Irregular bento grid rather than repeated equal-width cards.
- Small and large tiles create hierarchy.
- A large visual can span multiple logical units.
- Text is minimal and integrated into the visual block.

### Card geometry and spacing

- Very rounded rectangles.
- Compact gaps.
- Tiles behave as a connected visual family rather than independent bordered cards.
- Large feature tile establishes an anchor.

### Color/material

- Coral, purple, blue, yellow, mint, aqua, and orange.
- Soft pastel saturation with enough contrast between neighboring blocks.
- No heavy outline dependence.
- Matte clay-like rendering.

### Typography/navigation

- Very little text.
- Category name is the primary label.
- Typography is simple because imagery carries most of the personality.

### Imagery / dimensional treatment

- Strongest 3D/clay reference in the set.
- Objects appear to sit above or partly escape their tiles.
- Shadows establish depth but stay soft.

### Motion cues

Static reference only. Suitable motion: 1–3px hover lift, slight object tilt/scale, no looping bounce.

### Responsive implication

- Bento spans must collapse intentionally, not merely squeeze.
- Feature tile can become full-width first.
- Decorative object overflow must not create page horizontal overflow.
- Text cannot depend on fixed image placement.

### Adopt

- bento/asymmetric section composition,
- category-colored blocks,
- soft 3D/clay language,
- feature tile hierarchy,
- sparse labels,
- playful depth.

### Adapt for UniStore

Potential categories/contexts:

- ร้านค้า,
- แคมเปญ,
- สินค้า,
- คำสั่งซื้อ,
- ชำระเงิน,
- รับสินค้า,
- campus merchandise/product groups.

Use the visual system to support navigation and discovery, not as decoration alone.

### Reject

- random category colors with no system,
- overflowing objects that cover labels/actions,
- making operational/admin queues into toy-like bento grids,
- 3D assets so large that they harm load time or readability.

## 8. Shared language across all five references

The common direction is **not** “make every page colorful.” The shared language is:

1. **Large visual zones** instead of many small bordered boxes.
2. **Rounded geometry** with visibly larger radii than the v2 workbench.
3. **Layered surfaces**: background field → section block → floating card/image.
4. **Visual hierarchy through scale and composition**, not through borders alone.
5. **Product/illustration-first presentation** on customer surfaces.
6. **Vivid accent colors on soft foundations**.
7. **Asymmetry/bento rhythm** to prevent repetitive dashboard grids.
8. **Compact navigation** that does not overpower page content.
9. **Soft elevation and controlled gradients** as depth tools.
10. **3D/clay/dimensional assets** as a brand signature, especially in discovery/hero/category contexts.
11. **Short copy** around visual content.
12. **Responsive simplification**: fewer decorations, simpler spans, protected CTAs, no page overflow.

## 10. Adopt / Adapt / Reject summary

### Adopt

- expressive visual hero and section framing,
- bento/asymmetric rhythm,
- image-first commerce cards,
- soft 3D/clay objects,
- vivid accents over soft neutral foundations,
- purple/indigo as a strong brand anchor,
- sky/mint/coral/orange/yellow as controlled supporting accents,
- larger radii,
- soft depth,
- compact navigation,
- stronger display typography hierarchy,
- playful category/discovery presentation.

### Adapt

- customer pages receive the strongest expression,
- transaction pages use the brand language without hiding state/next action,
- org/admin pages use color/depth mainly for grouping and task priority,
- gradients are tokenized and limited to major visual zones,
- shadows communicate layering rather than decorating every panel,
- 3D objects are optimized static assets where possible,
- Thai typography stays readable and is not forced into unsuitable Latin display fonts.

### Reject

- pixel-for-pixel copying,
- copied proprietary artwork,
- fake ratings/testimonials/metrics,
- glass everywhere,
- gradients on every component,
- huge hero treatment on operational pages,
- toy-like admin queues,
- continuous bounce/parallax,
- mouse-follow effects,
- WebGL without a justified product need,
- visual decoration that covers real status, price, evidence, or actions,
- low-contrast pastel text,
- desktop-only layouts,
- page-level overflow.

## 13. Phase 2 verified `ui-ux-pro-max` research

This section stores only results that were actually returned by the installed local skill. Later phases should re-run narrower searches when a route archetype needs more specific evidence.

### 13.1 Style direction

**Query:** `playful premium ecommerce bento claymorphism dimensional layering rounded marketplace`  
**Domain:** `style`

Returned:

- **Dimensional Layering**
  - depth, overlapping layers, floating cards and spatial hierarchy,
  - neutral foundation + brand accent,
  - low implementation cost according to the skill,
  - but high accessibility risk if contrast/focus/reduced-motion are ignored.

**Query:** `3d clay illustration product cards performance static assets`  
**Domain:** `style`

Relevant returned styles:

- **Dimensional Layering**
  - useful for product showcases and layered card systems.
- **3D Product Preview**
  - appropriate for high-value interactive product visualization,
  - explicitly high performance cost and high implementation complexity.
- **Tactile Digital / Deformable UI**
  - press deformation and tactile feedback can work for playful consumer controls,
  - should be reduced to small press feedback rather than spring-heavy UI.
- **3D & Hyperrealism**
  - immersive but high performance/accessibility cost,
  - unsuitable as the default UniStore rendering strategy.
- **Bento Box Grid**
  - varied 1x1 / 2x1 / 2x2 spans,
  - 16–24px rounded geometry,
  - responsive 4 → 2 → 1 column guidance,
  - low performance and accessibility risk when implemented with CSS Grid.
- **Motion-Driven**
  - supports microinteractions but the skill suggests 300–400ms and heavier animation;
    UniStore should adapt this downward to shorter, quieter motion.
- **Data-Dense Dashboard**
  - appropriate evidence for operational screens:
    compact spacing, dense readable type, overflow-aware tables, sticky headers.
- **Claymorphism**
  - soft 3D, chunky rounded elements and pastel color,
  - useful as an illustration/material reference,
  - reject its default 3–4px thick-border treatment for the main application UI.

### 13.2 Product archetype evidence

**Query:** `marketplace ecommerce storefront product cards product detail checkout status`  
**Domain:** `product`

Relevant returned product types:

- **E-commerce**
  - primary recommendation: **Vibrant & Block-based**,
  - secondary: Aurora UI + Motion-Driven,
  - landing: Feature-Rich Showcase,
  - palette: brand primary + success green.
- **Classifieds / Buy-Sell**
  - primary: Flat Design + Vibrant & Block-based,
  - secondary: Minimalism/Swiss + **Bento Box Grid**,
  - listing emphasis: filter-heavy discovery,
  - palette: neutral background + category/state accents.
- **Digital Products/Downloads**
  - Vibrant & Block-based + Motion-Driven,
  - Bento Box Grid appears as a secondary recommendation.
- **Inventory & Stock Management**
  - Flat Design + Minimalism/Swiss,
  - data-dense dashboard recommendation,
  - functional status colors.
- **E-commerce Luxury**
  - Liquid Glass / Glassmorphism + 3D/Hyperrealism appeared,
  - this is evidence for premium depth only; UniStore should **not** inherit the glass-heavy treatment because it conflicts with readability/performance goals.

**Adoption conclusion:** the skill independently reinforces the user references: customer commerce should use **Vibrant & Block-based + selective Bento**, while operational inventory/management should remain more restrained and data-dense.

### 13.3 Typography evidence

**Query:** `thai modern ecommerce`  
**Domain:** `typography`

Returned:

1. **Thai Modern — Noto Sans Thai / Noto Sans Thai**
   - modern, readable, multilingual, accessible,
   - explicitly recommended for Thai sites.
2. **E-commerce Clean — Rubik / Nunito Sans**
   - visually relevant Latin reference, but not a safe Thai default.
3. Luxury/editorial Latin pairings and geometric Latin pairings also appeared.

**Decision:** keep **Noto Sans Thai** as the canonical Thai UI family. Do not introduce a Latin-only display font that creates Thai fallback mismatch. Playfulness should come from scale, weight, color, shape, and illustration.

### 13.4 Color evidence

**Query:** `marketplace ecommerce purple indigo orange teal`  
**Domain:** `color`

Relevant returned palettes:

- **Marketplace (P2P)**
  - primary `#7C3AED`,
  - secondary `#A78BFA`,
  - transaction accent green,
  - very light purple background.
- **E-commerce**
  - success/commerce green primary,
  - orange urgency/accent.
- Other returned combinations reinforce blue/teal/orange as compatible secondary families.

**Decision:** use marketplace purple/indigo as the v3 brand anchor candidate, while preserving green for transactional success and using orange/coral as controlled emphasis rather than making every semantic state decorative.

These values are **research candidates**, not final tokens until Phase 4.

### 13.5 Responsive and touch evidence

**Query:** `responsive bento grid mobile overflow touch target`  
**Domain:** `ux`

Returned guidance:

- Touch target size is high-severity guidance; the skill distinguishes mobile platform guidance from WCAG web minimums.
- Wide tables should use horizontal scrolling or a card alternative.
- Mobile layouts need enlarged touch targets.
- Adjacent touch targets should keep roughly 8px or more separation.
- The skill recommends mobile-first responsive composition.

**Decision:** v3 bento composition must be designed to collapse intentionally; it must not be a desktop grid squeezed down with fixed spans.

### 13.6 Checkout / transaction evidence

**Query:** `checkout validation payment status next action`  
**Domain:** `ux`

Returned:

- **Submit Feedback** — loading followed by success/error state.
- **Inline Validation** — on-blur validation for most fields.
- **Contextual Live Badge Updates** — announce one meaningful atomic status rather than many competing live regions.
- **Content Jumping** — reserve space/aspect ratio for async media, badges, validation and skeleton replacement.
- **Focusable Error Summary** — focus after failed submit, link each summary item to the invalid field, retain inline errors.

**Decision:** all visual changes to checkout/payment/order forms must preserve the v2 validation/focus architecture.

### 13.7 Operational queue evidence

**Query:** `dense operational dashboard queue status next action`  
**Domain:** `ux`

The search returned:

- **Submit Feedback**
- **Contextual Live Badge Updates**

The local UX database did **not** return a richer queue-layout rule for this exact query. Therefore queue hierarchy will combine:
- the verified data-dense style result,
- external commerce-admin references below,
- and the existing task-first behavioral requirements.

Do not claim the skill returned a queue-specific visual pattern that it did not.

## 16. Phase 2 research conclusion

The combined evidence supports a deliberate split:

### Customer visual layer

Use:

- vibrant block-based composition,
- selective bento/asymmetry,
- image-first commerce,
- soft 3D/clay assets,
- larger radii and stronger section atmosphere,
- controlled gradients and elevation,
- compact, low-chrome navigation.

### Transaction layer

Use the same brand language at lower intensity:

- stable layout,
- explicit product/order summary,
- authoritative totals/status,
- strong primary action,
- low-distraction forms,
- persistent validation/error recovery.

### Operations / platform layer

Modernize without turning operations into marketing:

- resource-list/index hierarchy,
- compact filters close to the data they affect,
- predictable action placement,
- visible scope/context,
- calm surfaces and fewer decorative objects,
- dense readable rows,
- task priority and next action before decorative composition.

### Media strategy

The refs justify a dimensional visual identity, but not a blanket WebGL strategy. Default to optimized static/generated clay/3D assets and CSS layering. Interactive 3D is opt-in only when it materially helps product understanding.

This phase does **not** finalize the design tokens. Phase 3 will synthesize these findings into a single art direction, and Phase 4 will turn that direction into the canonical design-system rules.

## 17. Phase 3 — Playful Campus Commerce v3 art direction

This is the **synthesis layer** between the reference/research evidence and the canonical design system that will be rewritten in Phase 4.

It does not replace `.interface-design/system.md` yet. Phase 4 must translate this direction into the single canonical token/component/layout authority.

All five canonical Pinterest URLs were re-opened at the start of this phase and returned **HTTP 200 image/jpeg**.

### 17.1 North-star statement

**UniStore Hub should feel like a contemporary campus marketplace with the visual confidence of a consumer commerce brand and the task clarity of a modern operations product.**

The user-facing side should feel:

- colorful without becoming childish,
- soft and dimensional without becoming skeuomorphic,
- premium without becoming luxury/minimal to the point of emptiness,
- playful without reducing trust in payment/order flows,
- visually rich without inventing marketplace content.

The operations side should feel:

- visibly related to the storefront,
- current and polished,
- compact and predictable,
- task-first rather than decorative,
- quieter than customer discovery but not like a legacy grey admin panel.

### 17.2 Brand personality

Target personality:

- **Playful:** 7/10
- **Premium:** 7/10
- **Friendly:** 8/10
- **Visual:** 8/10
- **Technical:** 4/10 on customer surfaces, 7/10 on operations
- **Minimal:** 5/10
- **Motion:** 3/10
- **Customer density:** 4–5/10
- **Transaction density:** 5–6/10
- **Operations density:** 8/10
- **Platform governance density:** 8/10

The product should not look like a children’s app, fintech casino, gaming UI, or generic dashboard template.

### 17.3 Expression spectrum by product mode

The same design language changes intensity depending on the job.

| Surface type | Expression level | Visual behavior |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Landing / marketplace discovery | 9/10 | strongest color, bento, illustration, dimensional layering |
| Store/product discovery | 8/10 | image-first cards, category color, layered product media |
| Product detail | 7/10 | premium media stage + clear purchase hierarchy |
| Login/register | 7/10 | branded split/composed environment, calm form surface |
| Checkout/order creation | 6/10 | branded but deliberately quieter and stable |
| Customer tracking/payment/pickup | 5–6/10 | state/next-action first, restrained dimensional framing |
| Org select/dashboard/settings | 5/10 | modern grouped surfaces, restrained bento summaries |
| Catalog/staff/campaign management | 4–5/10 | compact branded editors, minimal decorative media |
| Orders/payments/pickups queues | 3–4/10 | task-first resource list, status, timestamps, evidence, action |
| Audit / Platform users | 2–3/10 | highest restraint, strong scope and read-only/data clarity |
| Platform governance | 3–4/10 | authority and pending decisions first, brand language second |

### 17.4 Signature visual system

UniStore v3 should be recognizable through a **combination** of these signatures, not one gimmick repeated everywhere.

#### Signature A — Ambient commerce canvas

Customer pages should sit inside a soft colored environment rather than a flat grey/white document.

Use:

- warm off-white or softly tinted base,
- pale lavender / sky / mint environmental zones,
- large color fields behind content groups,
- occasional dark indigo premium section for contrast.

Avoid wrapping every individual section in a bordered card.

#### Signature B — Block + bento composition

Use varied composition to create hierarchy:

- 1×1 utility tiles,
- 2×1 feature tiles,
- large visual anchor blocks,
- image-first product tiles,
- compact category tiles,
- occasional overlap between artwork and its containing block.

Bento is a composition tool, not a mandatory layout for every page.

Operations should borrow the grouping logic but not the playful irregularity when it reduces scan speed.

#### Signature C — Soft dimensional media

Primary brand media should use:

- soft 3D/clay-inspired objects,
- product cutouts,
- abstract commerce/campus objects,
- subtle cast shadows,
- simple material lighting,
- visual depth without requiring real-time 3D.

Default technical strategy:

1. optimized WebP/PNG/SVG or generated static art,
2. CSS layering where sufficient,
3. interactive 3D only after a concrete product-value justification.

#### Signature D — Product-first commerce cards

Where product media exists, it should dominate the card.

Preferred order:

1. product/media zone,
2. product/store/campaign identity,
3. authoritative price/state,
4. concise supporting metadata,
5. one primary next action.

Do not place large paragraphs above the product image.

#### Signature E — Rounded but structured geometry

The references support visibly larger geometry than v2.

Direction:

- controls remain ergonomic and not cartoonishly round,
- content cards become medium/large radius,
- major environment/hero blocks may use the largest radius,
- pills remain for real chip/status semantics,
- not every surface should use the exact same radius.

Exact radius tokens belong to Phase 4.

#### Signature F — Soft elevation with purpose

Depth communicates:

- media floating above a block,
- selected/active overlay,
- dialog/drawer priority,
- premium hero composition,
- primary card hierarchy.

Depth should **not** be used to decorate every row or field.

#### Signature G — Low-chrome navigation

Navigation should feel lighter than the content:

- compact desktop header,
- restrained active-state treatment,
- clear account/scope actions,
- customer navigation visually quieter than hero/product media,
- org/platform navigation predictable and efficient.

#### Signature H — Strong Thai type hierarchy

Use Noto Sans Thai as the family.

Create contemporary character through:

- larger display scale,
- shorter headings,
- stronger weight contrast,
- tighter grouping,
- controlled line length,
- deliberate color hierarchy.

Do not introduce Latin-only display fonts that cause Thai fallback mismatch.

## 18. Color direction before tokenization

Phase 4 will choose exact final values. Phase 3 locks the **relationships**.

### Brand anchor

Primary candidate family:

- violet / indigo,
- inspired by the Marketplace skill palette and R1/R3/R5.

Purple/indigo should signal **UniStore identity**, not semantic success.

### Supporting expressive families

Use selectively:

- sky / cyan — discovery, ambient depth,
- mint / teal — freshness and secondary environmental fields,
- coral / warm orange — attention, campaign energy, visual warmth,
- soft yellow — category/illustrative accent,
- lavender — secondary brand field.

### Semantic colors remain separate

Success, warning, danger, and info must retain semantic meaning.

Do not recolor them merely to match a category or hero illustration.

### Neutral foundation

Use a warm/soft off-white rather than a cold enterprise grey as the main light customer canvas.

Operational pages can move closer to neutral white/inset surfaces while preserving the same brand hue family.

## 19. Gradient policy

Gradients are **allowed again in v3**, but only where they create a meaningful visual environment.

### Allowed

- marketplace hero backgrounds,
- large campaign/category feature blocks,
- illustration backdrops,
- ambient section transitions,
- occasional premium dark-indigo visual field,
- subtle media placeholders.

### Avoid

- routine input backgrounds,
- tables,
- every card,
- status badges,
- destructive/error states,
- dense queue rows,
- large areas behind long-form operational text.

### Rule

A page should normally have only a small number of gradient-bearing visual zones visible at once. Gradient is a section/media treatment, not the default surface token.

---

## 20. Depth and shadow policy

Use a simple depth hierarchy:

1. **Base:** no shadow; canvas and ordinary grouped surfaces.
2. **Raised:** soft short shadow for floating commerce cards/media.
3. **Overlay:** stronger but still soft depth for dialogs/drawers or intentionally overlapping visual layers.

Avoid:

- hard black shadows,
- multiple competing shadow styles,
- shadows around every operational row,
- inset clay effects on ordinary form fields.

The clay reference informs **brand assets and tactile moments**, not the default control rendering.

---

## 21. Illustration and media strategy

### 21.1 Asset classes

#### A. Brand-environment assets

Examples:

- shopping bag,
- parcel/box,
- campus merchandise object,
- card/payment token metaphor,
- pickup bag,
- abstract rounded geometry.

Use in:

- landing hero,
- discovery bento,
- authentication brand panel,
- major empty states.

#### B. Category/product fallback assets

Use when the backend has no product/store image.

Requirements:

- category-aware but generic,
- no fake product photograph,
- visually related to the v3 palette,
- should not imply a specific product that does not exist.

#### C. Transaction helper assets

Small contextual illustrations can support:

- payment upload,
- order complete/empty,
- pickup ready,
- notifications empty state.

They must remain secondary to authoritative state/action.

### 21.2 Informative vs decorative

Informative product imagery:

- meaningful alt text from actual product/store context.

Purely decorative brand/ambient objects:

- empty alt or presentation-hidden,
- must not capture pointer events,
- must not block focus/actions,
- must not create horizontal page overflow.

### 21.3 Performance

Default:

- optimized static asset,
- explicit aspect ratio/reserved size,
- responsive sizing,
- lazy-load below the fold,
- avoid unnecessary full-resolution art on mobile.

Do not add Three.js/Spline runtime just because the visual refs contain 3D-looking objects.

## 22. Motion direction

Motion should make the interface feel tactile, not animated for its own sake.

### Preferred

- hover lift: very small,
- press scale/compression: subtle,
- image reveal/fade: short,
- panel/dialog transition: short,
- status/update transition: stable and non-disruptive.

Target interaction timing:

- approximately **150–250ms** for common UI feedback.

### Avoid

- continuous floating/bobbing required to understand the page,
- mouse-follow effects,
- scroll-jacking,
- multi-layer parallax,
- exaggerated spring/bounce,
- animation on dense operational rows,
- motion that changes layout height unexpectedly.

Always provide reduced-motion behavior.

## 23. Composition rules by archetype

### 23.1 Landing / marketplace discovery

Structure direction:

- low-chrome nav,
- large visual hero/environment,
- short Thai headline,
- discovery/search/category bridge,
- bento category/store/campaign zone,
- product/store imagery,
- real backend-driven marketplace content.

Avoid generic SaaS sections such as repeated feature-card rows with marketing filler.

### 23.2 Store / campaign / product listing

Structure direction:

- image-forward cards,
- stronger media ratio,
- varied feature/listing block sizes only when backed by real priority,
- concise title/price/state,
- filters/search visually integrated with discovery.

### 23.3 Product detail

Structure direction:

- large media stage,
- store/campaign context visible but secondary,
- product title,
- variant/state,
- price / estimated total,
- primary order action,
- supporting facts below.

The page should feel like commerce, not a record-detail form.

### 23.4 Authentication

Structure direction:

- branded visual panel/environment,
- calm dedicated form surface,
- short copy,
- strong but not oversized primary action,
- password-manager/autocomplete semantics unchanged.

### 23.5 Customer transaction flows

Use:

- stable single task frame,
- product/order summary,
- authoritative status,
- one next action,
- lower illustration intensity,
- clear totals and timestamps.

Brand atmosphere is allowed around the flow; it must not compete with the transaction.

### 23.6 Org dashboard/home

Use:

- selected bento/grouped summary blocks,
- real actionable counts only,
- current organization/scope obvious,
- shortcut/task grouping,
- no generic KPI-card wall.

### 23.7 Operational resource pages

Use:

- page title/scope/action,
- filters/search,
- resource list/table,
- status + identifier + important amount/time,
- next valid action,
- selected/detail pane where useful.

Brand expression comes from surface geometry, spacing, subtle accent fields, focus/selected treatment—not from floating 3D decorations.

### 23.8 Platform governance

Use:

- unmistakable Platform scope,
- pending/attention work first,
- restrained brand layer,
- confirmation/recovery clarity,
- read-only user/audit areas visually calmer than actionable governance.

## 24. Anti-patterns for v3

The following are explicitly rejected.

### Visual

- returning to a flat grey legacy-admin appearance,
- thin grey border around every block,
- equal-size 3-card grids used by habit,
- giant empty marketing hero on operations pages,
- every surface using the same radius,
- every surface using a shadow,
- every section using a gradient,
- full-page glassmorphism,
- fake frosted transparency behind text-heavy content,
- random category colors with no palette relationship,
- ornamental 3D object on every screen,
- copying the Pinterest layouts exactly.

### Commerce

- fake ratings,
- fake review counts,
- fake bestseller labels,
- fake user counts,
- fake seller verification,
- fake sales numbers,
- invented product photos.

### Interaction

- hover-only essential controls,
- mouse-follow effects,
- continuous parallax,
- scroll-jacking,
- inaccessible drag-only UI,
- touch targets reduced for visual compactness,
- hiding status/action clarity to preserve a clean composition.

### Operations

- playful bento layouts that break scanning order,
- large illustration blocks above urgent queues,
- marketing copy inside operational pages,
- metric cards with no action or decision value,
- semantic status represented by color alone.

## 25. What v3 keeps from v2

The visual system changes substantially, but these v2 improvements are retained:

- backend-authoritative state,
- task/next-action clarity,
- semantic timestamps and numeric presentation,
- complex-form error summaries,
- inline validation,
- focus recovery,
- dialog confirmation for risky actions,
- contextual live status updates,
- direct-upload contract,
- responsive overflow protections,
- readable operational density,
- permission/scope clarity,
- static-export architecture.

The v2 **behavioral UX** remains a foundation. The v2 **restrained visual language** is what v3 replaces.
